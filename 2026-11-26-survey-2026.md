# Qubes OS User Survey 2026

In June and July this year (2026, for anyone reading this in the post-apocalyptic future) I conducted a Qubes OS User Survey on behalf of the entire Qubes OS team. The response was absolutely amazing: 1 855 participants completed the survey, of whom 1 322 were current Qubes OS users (referred to below as Current Users), 284 former Qubes OS users (Former Users) and 249 people who haven't (yet) used Qubes OS (Non-Users). This is a slightly smaller number than during our last survey in 2020, when about 2 100 participants filled out the survey, but that time, there were only 1 130 current Qubes OS users – and 2020 was not exactly a typical year, with many more people interested in just filling out surveys online due to limited other available entertainment.

![Pie chart of survey participants by user group](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_1.png)

We are very thankful to everyone who shared their opinions and experiences of Qubes OS with us – we hope to use the data collected in the survey to make Qubes better aligned with what its users actually want and need, and make it more accessible to more people who want or need to use it.

Before we begin with survey results, a little note on the processing of results. No tracking of participants was performed at any point, thus no information regarding source URL, IP addresses, browser fingerprints etc. was gathered – still, some (31) duplicate results could be easily recognised and thus were removed from the data set. Furthermore, an attempt to prompt-inject the survey results processing was discarded. All processing was done by a carbon-based intelligence (the one writing these words), not by stochastic parrots, so: nice try, but no, please do not do this again.

All questions in the survey except for the "Are you using Qubes?" were optional.

## Main Takeaways

If you don’t want to read the whole thing, the key findings of the survey are:

- Qubes OS is amazing, but can be extremely annoying to configure.

- GPU acceleration and better VPN qube setup are sorely needed.

- Hardware compatibility is a problem – both because not enough hardware is fully supported by Qubes, but also because compatible hardware is increasingly expensive.

- Documentation and tutorials need work, and Qubes can do a lot of amazing things but it can be hard to discover them.

- People who love Qubes really love it!

## Survey Participants

The first demographic question we asked was "Which, if any, of the following traits describe you?" To no great surprise, most of our users are IT professionals – over 60% of people who are current Qubes OS users self-describe as IT professionals, and further 30% are open-source contributors (thank you!). There are also many academics and activists (25% and 20%). 5% of our users describe themselves as journalists.

![Which traits describe you, by user group](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_2.png)

We asked our users a couple more questions about them; over 80% of Current and Former Qubes OS users have a reliable Internet connection, while only 60% of Non-Users do. It would seem like issues such as smaller download sizes might be more important to people interested in Qubes OS than to Current Users – or maybe that big download sizes can be a deterrent to people trying Qubes OS out.

![Internet access, by user group](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_3.png)

We also asked about preference for using GUI over CLI and vice versa:

![Preference for GUI vs CLI configuration, by user group](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_4.png)

It’s clear that most Qubes OS users are tinkerers at heart – almost no one wanted to have someone else do their configuration. The answers were mostly similar across different self-identified categories, with only two small outliers: IT professionals prefer GUI-only tools less often than users overall, but the majority is still comfortable with either GUI or CLI (62%), while journalists prefer GUI tools more often than other groups of users (40% vs 28%). It’s clear that most users appreciate existence of both GUI and CLI and we shouldn’t give up on either (and don’t worry, we absolutely will not).

## Discovering Qubes OS

As a project, we are not great at marketing – Qubes OS is really cool, but we are not that great at telling this to people who are not already convinced. Thus, we decided to ask – how do people learn about Qubes OS?

![Where respondents first learned about Qubes OS](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_5.png)

The most common answer was a generic "looking for information about security online" or "Internet"; however, the second-most common answer was YouTube (with particularly common mentions of channels such as Mental Outlaw, Techlore, Network Chuck, PafLeGeek, Side of Burritos, Switched to Linux and The Hated One). This came as a big surprise to the Qubes OS team which consists largely of people who don’t really watch YouTube at all and were quite astonished to hear about Qubes being mentioned there. Another common way people learned about Qubes OS was "from friends / coworkers / other social contact" – more common, in fact, than learning about it from Edward Snowden, which was the most popular source people mentioned in the previous survey.

Furthermore, three people mentioned TikTok, and to this I can only say that never have I felt older – but I’m glad we have also reached this territory so unfamiliar to me!

To the surprise of absolutely no one, security is the most commonly mentioned trait across all groups (and we try hard to keep it good). But there are some other interesting patterns in the data:

- Among Current Users, compartmentalization is almost as important as security; users who care about compartmentalization left longer and more involved comments, explaining how Qubes compartmentalization improves their ability to work, helps with the cognitive load of operating a computer and is, in general, a very important feature. This is something that I don’t think we talk about enough in our documentation.

- Similarly, network management is an absolute killer feature for many users; easy switching between fully offline qubes and qubes with different net qubes (e.g. different VPNs) is a powerful tool.

- Surprisingly, many users praised Qubes UX and improvements in the graphical user interface that occurred over the last couple of years – this makes this writer, who is also responsible for Qubes UX, very happy!

- Current Users emphasize ease of maintaining different environments (different templates, different operating systems, different installed packages), experimenting with potentially unsafe or unknown programs/devices and being able to easily revert to a known secure environment.

- Non-Users emphasize privacy, even though we do not really talk much about it in our messaging. This might partially be related to the common conflation of security and privacy, or maybe to ways we talk about security that might be unclear to someone?

A good conclusion of this question is that Qubes OS is pretty cool, but we aren’t that good at telling people who don’t use it about it. And thank you all for your kind words – it was a delight to read them!

Unlike in the previous survey in 2020, only a single-digit number of respondents accused us of being government plants, which is, I guess, progress?

| Topic                  | Mentions | Description                                                                                                  |
|------------------------|:--------:|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| hardware compatibility | 29 (12%) | difficult to find compatible hardware, problems with drivers or various devices (excluding suspend problems) |
| performance            | 19 (8%)  | bad system performance, resource consumption, RAM demands                                                    |

GPU acceleration is by and large the biggest problem for Current Users. The runner-up (performance problems) might partially be related, but also includes issues such as high RAM consumption, high CPU temperatures and bad battery life. The current sorry state of the global supply chain means that hardware compatibility issues (a problem mentioned across all user groups) are exacerbated. All other problems, while important, pale in comparison to GPU / performance / hardware compatibility. There is some positive development with regard to GPU acceleration (<https://media.ccc.de/v/qubesos26-110896-virtio-gpu-is-coming-to> ), so there is hope for improvement in this area. Still, hardware remains one of the key barriers to Qubes OS accessibility.

And when it comes to the other problems, there is also some hope.

- Slow backups are being improved with the incorporation of Wyng backup into Qubes OS (<https://codeberg.org/tasket/wyng-util-qubes>).

- Difficult configuration was also discussed a lot at the Qubes OS Summit 2026, and there are ideas and the willingness to implement them.

Work on improving the UX and UI always continues, too. Interestingly, a noticeable number of former users complained about the UI being ugly or outdated – not saying it was unusable, just explicitly, aesthetically unpleasant. Some of those responses contained a strong preference for the more "modern" look of GNOME, which sadly is unlikely to become part of Qubes OS officially supported desktop environments, as its approach to window decorations isn’t very compatible with ours.

Many topics mentioned in the wishlist are mirrored by the dislikes mentioned in the section above – and as said there, there is hope for improvement. A particularly important topic that wasn’t discussed above is the need for a better setup experience: very many Current Users would like setting up all sorts of useful features to be less painful. We completely agree (and there is work happening on the topic, see https://media.ccc.de/v/qubesos26-110886-design-session-meta-app). We also hope that support for KDE will improve (especially with Wayland integration proceeding) and UX will consistently become better and better.

Some ideas mentioned by survey participants merit mention:

- **will suspend ever work?** Working on it! Sadly, the compatibility situation is ... complex, and we often discover new and exciting Xen or kernel bugs related to suspend.

- **template inheritance** (templates inheriting from templates)? This is unlikely to happen. The complexity and possible bugs would make this feature a big can of worms... but for an approximation of it, the bind-dirs feature might be helpful (<https://doc.qubes-os.org/en/latest/user/advanced-topics/bind-dirs.html>).

- **sway/i3 as default desktop environment**? No, this is a choice that works great for some people, but requires a lot of additional configuration for most users. But we will always try to be compatible with i3 / sway (when Wayland comes).

- **rewrite everything in Rust**? (Human-written, well-designed) patches welcome! More seriously, some parts of the system are / will be written in Rust, while for others, the cost of rewriting brings little benefit.

- **video tutorials**? Please, help! In this area, we would really welcome community support, as nobody on the team is very good with video.

## Habits of Using Qubes OS

This section applies only to Current Users. The main goal was to get an idea of how Qubes OS is used in practice – for what? How often? What are people’s typical setups? – so that we know what typical usage patterns are. How many qubes should be easily viewable in the menu? Is it realistic to assume people have X amount of free RAM? All those questions can be more easily answered with this data.

### Using Qubes in daily life

The first two questions: "how often do you use Qubes OS" and "do you use Qubes OS for professional / private purposes?" show us that most people who do use Qubes OS use it pretty intensely. It’s the daily driver for 68% of respondents, and further 20% use it every day or almost every day. Does this mean Qubes OS is just so amazing that people can’t help but use it all the time – or does it require so much time to set up and learn that only the most dedicated people persevere? I think both can play a part, but hopefully we’ll be able to move closer to "mostly column A" on this.

![How often respondents use Qubes OS](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_7.png)

![Whether Qubes OS is used for work or personal purposes](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_8.png)

Most – but not overwhelmingly so – survey participants use Qubes OS on a laptop. However, it’s clear we should think about our desktop users, too – 39% of users use Qubes OS on a desktop exclusively or partially.

![Laptop vs desktop usage of Qubes OS](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_9.png)

### Number of Qubes

On the team, we have people who have hundreds of qubes (also known as "GUI tools stress testers") and people who have a dozen. Thus, we were very curious what was common among our users.

![Number of templates in use](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_10.png)

![Number of app qubes in use](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_11.png)

![Number of disposable templates in use](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_12.png)

Clearly, when preparing the survey, I underestimated how many templates users have: nearly half (43%) replied that they use more than 7 templates, and 40% use between 4 and 7 templates. Similarly, almost a third of users have more than 20 app qubes, and about a third have between 11 and 20. Very clearly, we have to continue stress-testing Qubes OS with a lot of qubes.

Many users also have multiple disposable templates, with almost 60% having between 2 and 4 – and 9% having more than 10. Again, that’s a pointer to us: make the system perform and look good with a lot of qubes. It also suggests that perhaps the default installation should have more disposable templates: clearly, most people use more than the single default disposable.

### Hardware

As far as hardware goes, most users have a lot of RAM (2/3 having more than 32 GB) and quite a lot of disk space (over 2/3 have more than half a terabyte). However, the current supply chain situation means that we can’t rest on our laurels and decide Qubes OS can just ignore resource consumption.

![Amount of RAM](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_13.png)

![Amount of disk space](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_14.png)

More people than we feared (but fewer than we would like) are able to use suspend on their Qubes OS systems. 1/3 of all users just use it (which is quite optimistic, given the less-than-ideal state of modern suspend on Linux in general and Qubes OS in particular), but 20% would like to use it, if only it worked. 40% do not use suspend at all.

![Use of suspend](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_15.png)

### Templates

Unsurprisingly, an overwhelming majority of users uses the default templates, with a small lead given to Debian over Fedora. However, for a non-default choice, ArchLinux is extremely popular, with 148 mentions (which is 11% of all Current Users). NixOS is also quite popular, which is notable as it’s not available in the community repository. There is also a strong user base for Alpine, Gentoo, Ubuntu and Devuan.

![Template distributions in use](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_16.png)

### Features

The next question, about various Qubes OS features, was meant not only to tell us what is useful – it was also a question about how discoverable and familiar various default and non-default features are.

![Usage and awareness of selected Qubes OS features](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_17.png)

The biggest takeaways are that some built-in features are largely unknown to users – in particular, Qubes Video Companion is used only by 13% of Current Users, while at least 33% have no idea what it is. This means more people are familiar with a non-default, community feature Mirage Firewall than with the built-in Qubes Video Companion. We do need to do better at communicating all the cool things Qubes OS can do! Also, we clearly need a better setup for a VPN qube. More users use a VPN qube (which requires custom settings, following one of several community guides and general faffing about) than the built-in qube firewall or built-in PDF converter.

### Opening Applications

![How applications are opened in a running qube](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_18.png)

There is a little oops in this question: the intent of the Qube Manager option was the "run command in qube" right-click option, but it was obviously unclear to many respondents, judging from responses in the ‘Other’ field. I apologize for the lack of clarity!

It is clear that most users use Qubes App Menu and the Domains widget; at the very least, it suggests the menu and the widget are known and used by many users. Very few users use or are aware of XFCE-specific options – more users use i3-specific options, in fact, than XFCE-specific options. It might be that Qubes OS users are not very familiar with XFCE-specific configuration. A notable number of users mentioned using a Super + E shortcut, which seems to be a default in some flavours of Qubes OS templates – this was a surprise to some members of the team.

### Colours

![How qube label colours are assigned](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_19.png)

First things first: a lot of respondents want more options – 96 users used the "Other" field to mention that they want more label colours by default. And they will be happy to hear that more colours are indeed coming! (<https://github.com/QubesOS/qubes-artwork/pull/54>).

Interestingly, the default or documentation-recommended way of using colours – to denote security level – is common, but not the only approach users take. In particular, almost half of Current Users (49%) use colour to denote the area of activity / project the qube belongs to. Two rarer approaches were quite popular: vibes-based "how I feel about it" (for example blue for a Signal qube), and a very interesting idea of denoting networking (one colour for each net qube and its connected qubes). It’s – in my opinion – a thing we really could improve: better communicate that there are different ways of using qube labels to achieve security and convenience.

I would also like to shout out the user who has an LED strip under the monitor and sets it to the colour of fullscreen qubes – this is such a beautiful piece of overengineering!

### Preferred Desktop Environment

![Preferred desktop environment, by user group](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_20.png)

Among Current Users, XFCE is clearly dominant (unsurprisingly; it is, after all, the Qubes default, so users will be biased towards the thing they don’t have to change – however, I think it does say something that Non-Users and Former Users are significantly less enthused about XFCE). Strong support for KDE among Non-Users is also interesting; a lot of people mentioned it as their wishlist idea for Qubes OS, and as their favourite DE. There is a loud minority in strong support of i3/Sway and tiling desktop environments in general (which is another reason why it’s important to keep Qubes GUI tools compatible with them), and a big group of GNOME fans.

A noticeable minority of wishlist answers also pointed to wanting a more modern / aesthetically pleasing look, which could be KDE or perhaps better XFCE defaults?

## Notifications

The question "How clear do you usually find Qubes OS notifications, warnings and security-related messages?" was asked because we are (still) working on improving and re-working the Qube Manager – widgets interaction flow. And there were a lot of extremely helpful answers to it!

![Perceived clarity of Qubes OS notifications](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_21.png)

It was a relief to see that most users find Qubes OS notifications Very clear (23%) or Mostly clear (53%). However, a lot of problems were mentioned in the open question that deserve closer scrutiny.

The most commonly mentioned problem categories were unclear, insufficient or misleading notifications and annoying notifications, with particularly notable mentions of:

- Wanting a notification log (KDE actually offers one... but it’s not easy to find for Qubes users).

- Being annoyed by notification spam in certain situations (which is a sign we should look into batched notifications for some events, like "new device" when a USB stick is plugged in).

- Confusing disk space notifications.

- Confusing policy error notifications.

- Annoying and unclear "couldn’t connect to qrexec for 60s" error.

- Wanting a notification about a new Qubes version or a new Qubes Security Bulletin / XSA.

The general state of notifications isn’t bad – but there is definitely a lot of space for improvement.

## Configuring Qubes OS

Configuring Qubes OS can be an arduous journey (as the Qubes OS dislikes question showed). We are quite aware of that, so we are always looking for ways to improve that experience and make it less of a pain.

![Use of default app qubes](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_22.png)

It turns out that most users either use default app qubes or have used them in the past; there is clearly value in providing some set of defaults to build upon. In the open question "If you have an opinion: what set of default app qubes would you want to see on a fresh Qubes OS install?", a lot of users (over 700) gave their opinions, with the most common answer being "just stick with existing defaults". However, there were some very interesting proposals that merit discussion, in particular:

- an email qube,

- a banking qube,

- a video call qube with preconfigured microphone and camera auto-attach,

- a default VPN qube,

- easier default setup for sys-audio with Bluetooth.

There were also many proposals on changing the default applications (remove LibreOffice, add more LibreOffice, add this flavour of Firefox, remove Firefox, add Chromium, ensure there is absolutely no Chromium anywhere... ) – as we generally try to keep close to distribution defaults (<https://doc.qubes-os.org/en/latest/introduction/faq.html#could-you-please-make-my-preference-the-default>), it is unlikely that big changes will happen here.

![Interest in multiple disposable templates](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_23.png)

The majority of users would like to have multiple disposable templates – and I also think this is a very good idea, coming soon™ (that is, at some point in time).

A lot of people gave an answer to the open question "Do you have other comments on improving Qubes OS setup/initial configuration experience?". The most-mentioned topics were:

- Including tutorial or docs during install / on startup: which is a great idea that we definitely should work on introducing.

- Easier/automatic VPN setup: hopefully, work that is related to <https://media.ccc.de/v/qubesos26-110886-design-session-meta-app> will help here.

- Configuration sets depending on user needs: as above.

- Easier restore from backup after install: another good idea that might be a little complex to do in practice.

- Incorporating Salt/Ansible into the install process: that is pretty low on our to-do list – Salt/Ansible can be used on an installed Qubes OS system, and there is install automation.

- Easier configuration of 2FA during install: another extremely good idea.

- More templates / Windows available on install ISO: if for no other reason, then due to licensing, Windows cannot happen; the problem with other templates is that the ISO is already pretty bloated, and it is hard to add more things to it without making it difficult to use for many users.

## Accessibility

Unsurprisingly, most people who filled out this survey considered Qubes OS accessible to people like them, with the Non-Users group the least positive on the subject (but still mostly positive).

![Whether Qubes OS is considered accessible, by user group](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_24.png)

What was very interesting were the reasons users gave why Qubes wouldn’t be accessible to people like them – the biggest being hardware compatibility, with the time commitment a close second. Many other survey participants also mentioned the steep learning curve and complexity (especially current and former users). The answers to this question show us that most people who do start using Qubes OS do not consider it too intimidating – but it is hard on the system resources, unfortunately, and generally complex. I think making the setup less onerous would do a lot to reduce some of those barriers.

## Other Operating Systems

![Other operating systems used, by user group](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_25.png)

Most respondents use various Linux distributions, with Debian/Ubuntu distributions dominating the results, and a strong showing from RHEL/Fedora, Tails and ArchLinux. Interestingly, Windows has many more users than macOS. While among Non-Users there are relatively more mentions of Windows than of Linux-based systems, in the other groups Linux dominates.

Note: Some users complained that there wasn’t an "I only use Qubes" option – the intent was that if someone only used Qubes OS, they would leave the question empty, but it was not explicitly stated – apologies for the confusion!

Some users also mentioned using other operating systems like Slackware, DOS or TempleOS – they were omitted from the chart above for brevity’s sake.

## Breaking Points

It’s an important question: of those who used to use Qubes OS, but stopped, what was it that drove them away? Nearly 200 Former Users gave us their reasons, with the main answer groups being: the difficulty and time cost of using Qubes, hardware compatibility problems, GPU acceleration and bad performance.

![Reasons for stopping using Qubes OS](/attachment/posts/survey2026_chart_26.png)

Unsurprisingly, users who used Qubes OS mostly for personal/hobby purposes were more often driven away by lack of GPU acceleration, difficulty of using Qubes OS and desktop environment problems, while those who used it for work purposes were more discouraged by hardware problems, bad performance or lack of Windows support.
